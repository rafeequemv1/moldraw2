How it works

Three steps, nothing to draw by hand. The addon behaves like the Windows Snip tool, but the result is chemistry you can edit.

Click the MolDraw icon

From any tab: a paper on a publisher site, a PDF in the browser, lecture slides, Wikipedia, a supplier catalogue. The page dims and the cursor turns into a crosshair. The page is frozen so nothing scrolls under you.

Drag a box around the structure

Only the region you select is captured, at your screen's full resolution, exactly as you see it on screen — including anything inside modals or image lightboxes.

Open it in MolDraw

Mathpix chemistry OCR reads the crop and returns SMILES. MolDraw opens in a new tab with the structure fully editable: change substituents, add reagents, export to SVG, PNG, MOL or copy to Word.

What you get

Built for people who read papers all day and redraw far too many structures.

Capture

Snip, not screenshot

Click-and-drag selection with a live size read-out. Works on HiDPI displays and with browser zoom without offset. Esc cancels at any time.

Recognise

Mathpix chemistry OCR

Uses Mathpix's structure recognition to turn drawn molecules into SMILES, including multi-structure figures and reaction schemes.

Edit

Straight into MolDraw

The result opens in the editor you already use, with the SMILES available to copy. Reactions open in MolDraw's reaction mode.

Privacy

Nothing stored by MolDraw

The crop goes from your browser to Mathpix and back. MolDraw only receives the resulting SMILES in the URL. Your keys live in your browser profile.

Browsers

Chrome and Edge

Manifest V3 extension. Identical behaviour in Google Chrome and Microsoft Edge on Windows, macOS and Linux.

Shortcut

Alt + Shift + M

Start a capture without reaching for the toolbar. The keys can be changed in your browser's extension shortcuts.

Frequently asked

Does it work on PDFs?

Yes. For PDFs opened in the browser the addon captures the visible page and lets you drag the snip box over the structure.

Does it work in Microsoft Edge?

Yes. Edge is built on the same extension platform as Chrome, so the addon is identical there.

Why do I need a Mathpix account?

Mathpix provides the chemistry recognition (image → SMILES). Using your own key keeps the addon free and means MolDraw never sees your screenshots.

What if the structure is recognised incorrectly?

The structure opens in MolDraw where you can fix it in seconds. A tighter crop with only one structure and no caption gives the best results.

Will there be Word and PowerPoint addons?

Yes, they are in development. See the addons page.
